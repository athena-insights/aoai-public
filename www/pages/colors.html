Diverging scale

We use this diverging scale for questions that have two sides: darkest at each end and lighter toward the center. The purples are the concern, too-little, etc., side. The oranges are the opposite: excited, somewhat excited, too-much, etc. Each lighter step is its pole mixed halfway (50%) toward our paper, in OKLab. We use a gray for residuals, such as not sure.

TokenDerivationFor exampleValue
--neg-0Orange.Very excited.
--neg-1Somewhat excited.
--pos-1Somewhat concerned.
--pos-0Purple.Very concerned.
--resid-0--rung-65Not sure.

Parties

These are two special colors we use by convention for partisan charts. In general, they were chosen to match the strength of our brand purple and orange, their distance from each other under color-blindness tests, and proximity to recognizable red and blues.

TokenLineValue
--group-party-democratDemocrats.
--group-party-republicanRepublicans.

Category palette

We have a four-category palette for line charts that aren't a response (diverging scale colors) or a party (partisan colors), such as an age group. They are: our ink solid, our ink dashed, chart gray solid, chart gray dashed.

Margins of error

We create a trend line's margin of error from the line's own color at one opacity, --band-opacity, , over paper. A bar's margin of error is a whisker across its end, in our ink.
